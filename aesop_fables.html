Five Aesop fables rewritten in simple English for beginner learners, with vocabulary definitions and comprehension questions

Simple English Reading — Month 1

Aesop's Fables

Five famous stories, written in simple English. Underlined words have meanings you can learn. Read slowly and out loud.

Story One
The Tortoise and the Hare
A story about pride and hard work
Week 1–2 level

Once there was a tortoise and a hare. The hare could run very fast. He was proud of this. He often laughed at the tortoise. "You are so slow!" said the hare. "I am the fastest animal here."

One day, the tortoise said, "Let us have a race." The hare laughed. "A race? You are a tortoise! I will win easily." But the tortoise said, "We will see."

The race began. The hare ran very fast. He looked back and did not see the tortoise. He thought, "I have a lot of time. I will rest here." He sat down under a tree and fell asleep.

The tortoise did not stop. He walked slowly, but he did not rest. He walked and walked and walked. After a long time, the tortoise passed the sleeping hare. He kept walking.

When the hare woke up, he ran fast to the finish line. But when he arrived, the tortoise was already there. The tortoise won the race.

The lesson of this story
"Slow and steady wins the race." — It is better to work hard every day than to be lazy, even if you are very talented.
New words in this story
tortoise
noun
A slow animal with a very hard shell on its back. It lives on land.
"The tortoise walked slowly to the finish line."
hare
noun
An animal like a big rabbit. It has long ears and runs very fast.
"The hare ran quickly but fell asleep."
proud
adjective
Feeling very good about yourself — sometimes too much.
"He was proud because he was the fastest."
race
noun
A competition to see who is the fastest.
"Let us have a race to the big tree."
woke up
verb (past)
Stopped sleeping. Base form: wake up.
"He woke up very late in the morning."
steady
adjective
Continuing at the same speed without stopping.
"She walked at a steady pace all day."
Comprehension questions — write your answers
1. Who won the race — the hare or the tortoise?
2. Why did the hare fall asleep? Use "because" in your answer.
3. Write one sentence about the tortoise using "is" or "was".
4. What does proud mean? Write your own sentence with this word.
Story Two
The Lion and the Mouse
A story about kindness and helping each other
Week 1–2 level

One day, a big lion was sleeping under a tree. A small mouse was running and playing. The mouse did not see the lion. He ran over the lion's face. The lion woke up. He was very angry. He caught the mouse in his big paw.

"I will eat you!" said the lion. The mouse was very scared. "Please do not eat me," said the mouse. "I am very small. I am not a good meal for you. Please let me go. One day, I will help you." The lion laughed. "You? Help me? You are a small mouse. I am a great lion." But the lion was kind. He let the mouse go.

Some days later, the lion was walking in the forest. He stepped into a trap. A net fell on him. He could not move. He roared very loudly. He was angry and scared.

The mouse heard the lion. He ran to the lion quickly. "I will help you," said the mouse. The mouse used his small sharp teeth. He bit the ropes of the net. He bit and bit until the ropes broke. The lion was free.

"Thank you, little mouse," said the lion. "You were right. You did help me." The mouse smiled. "Even a small friend can be a good friend," he said.

The lesson of this story
"A small kindness can come back to help you." — Do not think someone is too small or too weak to help you. Everyone can help.
New words in this story
angry
adjective
Very unhappy and upset about something. Feeling strong emotion.
"The lion was angry when the mouse woke him up."
scared
adjective
Feeling afraid. Something makes you feel fear.
"The mouse was very scared of the big lion."
trap
noun
A device used to catch an animal. The animal cannot get out.
"The lion stepped into a trap in the forest."
roared
verb (past)
Made a very loud, deep sound. Lions and tigers roar. Base form: roar.
"The lion roared because he was trapped."
sharp
adjective
Having a very thin edge that can cut things easily. A knife is sharp.
"The mouse had small but very sharp teeth."
kindness
noun
The act of being friendly and helpful to other people or animals.
"The lion showed kindness when he let the mouse go."
Comprehension questions — write your answers
1. Why was the lion angry at the start of the story?
2. What did the mouse do to help the lion? Write two sentences.
3. Write a sentence using the word "scared".
4. Do you think the lion was right to let the mouse go? Write "Yes, because..." or "No, because..."
Story Three
The Boy Who Cried Wolf
A story about telling the truth
Week 2–3 level

There was a young boy. His job was to watch the sheep on a hill near his village. Every day, he sat on the hill and watched the sheep eat grass. It was a quiet and boring job.

One day, the boy was bored. He had an idea. "I will play a trick," he thought. He ran to the village and shouted, "Wolf! Wolf! A wolf is eating the sheep!" The people of the village ran up the hill with sticks. But there was no wolf. The boy laughed. The people were very angry. They went back to the village.

The next week, the boy did the same thing again. "Wolf! Wolf!" he shouted. Again, the people ran up the hill. Again, there was no wolf. The boy laughed again. The people were angrier this time. "Do not lie to us," they said.

Then, one day, a real wolf came. The wolf was big and fierce. It ran at the sheep. The boy was very scared. He ran to the village and shouted, "Wolf! Wolf! Please help me! A real wolf is here!"

But the people heard him and did not come. They thought he was lying again. The wolf ate many of the sheep. The boy sat on the hill alone and cried. Nobody came to help him.

The lesson of this story
"If you always lie, people will not believe you when you tell the truth." — Honesty is very important. When you lie, you lose the trust of others.
New words in this story
bored
adjective
Feeling unhappy because nothing interesting is happening. You have nothing to do.
"The boy was bored because the job was very quiet."
trick
noun
Something you do to make another person believe something that is not true.
"He played a trick on the people of the village."
lie
verb
To say something that you know is not true.
"Do not lie — always tell the truth."
fierce
adjective
Very dangerous, strong, and ready to attack. Describes a violent animal.
"The fierce wolf ran at the sheep."
trust
noun/verb
Believing that a person is honest and will not hurt you.
"The people lost their trust in the boy."
honesty
noun
The quality of always telling the truth and not lying.
"Honesty is one of the most important things in life."
Comprehension questions — write your answers
1. Why did the boy shout "Wolf!" the first time?
2. Why did the people not help the boy when the real wolf came?
3. Write a sentence with the word "bored". Use present simple.
4. Have you ever heard a story like this in your language? How is it the same? How is it different?
Story Four
The Ant and the Grasshopper
A story about planning and hard work
Week 3–4 level

It was summer. The sun was hot and the days were long. An ant was working very hard. Every day, she carried food to her home under the ground. She worked all morning and all afternoon. She was preparing for winter.

A grasshopper saw the ant. He was sitting on a leaf and singing. "Ant, why do you work so hard?" said the grasshopper. "It is summer! The sun is warm. Come and sing with me." The ant said, "I am working because winter is coming. I need food for the cold days." The grasshopper laughed. "Winter is far away. Do not worry. Come and play!"

The ant did not stop working. She carried food every day, all summer long. The grasshopper sang and played and ate. He did not work. He did not save any food.

Then winter came. The weather was cold and there was no food outside. The grasshopper was very hungry. He went to the ant's home and knocked on the door. "Please," he said. "I am very hungry and cold. I have no food. Can you help me?" The ant looked at the grasshopper. "I worked all summer," she said. "I told you to save food. You did not listen. You only played." Then she closed the door.

The grasshopper stood alone in the cold and thought about his mistake.

The lesson of this story
"Work hard today so you are ready for tomorrow." — It is important to plan and prepare. Do not only enjoy the good times and forget about difficulties that may come.
New words in this story
preparing
verb
Getting ready for something that will happen in the future. Base form: prepare.
"She was preparing dinner for the family."
grasshopper
noun
A green jumping insect. It makes a sound like singing. It lives in grass.
"The grasshopper sat on a leaf and sang all day."
save
verb
To keep something so you can use it later. To not use it all now.
"I save money every month for the future."
lazy
adjective
Not wanting to work or do things. Preferring to rest all the time.
"The grasshopper was lazy and did not save food."
mistake
noun
Something you do that is wrong or not good. An error.
"Not saving food was a big mistake."
hunger / hungry
noun / adjective
The feeling when you need food and have not eaten. "Hungry" = the adjective.
"I am hungry — I did not eat breakfast."
Comprehension questions — write your answers
1. What did the ant do all summer? Write two sentences using present simple.
2. Why was the grasshopper hungry in winter?
3. Write a sentence: "The grasshopper did not _____ food."
4. Do you think the ant was right to close the door? Write "I think..." in your answer.
Story Five
The Fox and the Grapes
A story about accepting the truth
Week 3–4 level

One hot afternoon, a fox was walking through a forest. He was very hungry. He looked everywhere for food but he could not find anything to eat. Then he saw something. High up on a tree, there was a big bunch of grapes. They were dark purple and they looked sweet and delicious.

"Those grapes look wonderful," said the fox. "I will eat them." He jumped up to reach the grapes. But the grapes were too high. He could not reach them. He tried again. He jumped as high as he could. He still could not reach the grapes. He tried again and again, many times. Each time, he failed.

The fox was tired. He was hot. He sat down on the ground and looked up at the grapes. He could not get them. He would never get them. He felt ashamed.

Then the fox stood up. He put his nose in the air. He looked at the grapes one more time. "Those grapes are probably sour anyway," he said. "I do not want them. They are not good grapes." He walked away with his head held high.

But really, the fox did want the grapes. And he did not know if they were sour or sweet. He only said they were sour because he could not have them.

The lesson of this story
"It is easy to say you do not want something when you cannot have it." — When we fail to get something, we sometimes say we did not want it anyway. But this is not honest — it is just a way to hide our disappointment.
New words in this story
grapes
noun (plural)
Small, round, juicy fruit that grow in a bunch. They can be green or purple.
"She bought a bunch of grapes from the market."
reach
verb
To stretch out your arm or body to touch or get something.
"I cannot reach the top shelf — it is too high."
sour
adjective
A sharp, unpleasant taste. Not sweet. Like a lemon or unripe fruit.
"This orange is very sour — it is not sweet."
ashamed
adjective
Feeling embarrassed and bad because you did something wrong or you failed.
"He was ashamed because he could not reach the grapes."
delicious
adjective
Tasting very good. Very enjoyable to eat or drink.
"The food at that restaurant is delicious."
disappointment
noun
The sad feeling you have when something you wanted does not happen.
"He felt great disappointment when he failed the exam."
Comprehension questions — write your answers
1. Why could the fox not get the grapes?
2. What did the fox say about the grapes? Why did he say this?
3. Write one sentence using the word "delicious".
4. Have you ever said something was bad because you could not have it? Write 2 sentences about it.
Story Six
The Crow and the Pitcher
A story about using your mind to solve problems
Week 2–3 level

It was a very hot summer day. A crow was flying over the land. She was very thirsty. She looked and looked for water. Then she saw a pitcher on the ground. She flew down and looked inside. There was water at the bottom. But the pitcher was tall and the water was very low. The crow put her beak inside. She could not reach the water. Her beak was too short.

The crow did not give up. She sat and thought. Then she had an idea. She looked around and saw many small stones. She picked up a stone in her beak and dropped it into the pitcher. Then she picked up another stone and dropped it in. She did this many times.

Slowly, the water began to rise. Each stone pushed the water a little higher. The crow kept dropping stones. She worked patiently and did not stop. After a long time, the water was high enough. The crow put her beak in and drank the cool water. She was very happy.

The lesson of this story
"Think carefully and use what you have." — When you have a problem, do not give up. Look around and think. Patience and a good idea can solve many problems.
New words in this story
pitcher
noun
A tall container for holding liquid. It has a handle and a narrow top opening.
"She filled the pitcher with cold water."
thirsty
adjective
Feeling that you need to drink water or liquid.
"I am very thirsty — can I have some water please?"
patiently
adverb
In a calm way, waiting without getting angry or giving up.
"She waited patiently for the bus to arrive."
rise
verb
To go up. To move from a lower place to a higher place.
"The water began to rise after the heavy rain."
give up
phrasal verb
To stop trying because something is too hard.
"Do not give up — keep trying and you will succeed."
beak
noun
The hard, pointed mouth of a bird. Birds use their beak to eat and drink.
"The crow used her beak to pick up the stone."
Comprehension questions — write your answers
1. Why could the crow not drink the water at first?
2. What was the crow's idea? Write two sentences.
3. Write a sentence using "give up". Use "do not" in your sentence.
4. Have you ever solved a problem with a good idea? Write 2 sentences about it.
Story Seven
The Wind and the Sun
A story about gentleness being stronger than force
Week 2–3 level

One day, the Wind and the Sun had an argument. "I am stronger than you," said the Wind. "No," said the Sun. "I am stronger." They could not agree. Then they saw a man walking on a road. He was wearing a warm coat.

"Let us have a contest," said the Sun. "The one who can make that man take off his coat is stronger." The Wind said yes. The Wind went first.

The Wind blew hard. He blew and blew with all his strength. The wind was very cold and very strong. But the man held his coat tightly. The harder the Wind blew, the more the man held his coat. The Wind blew for a long time. But the man did not take off his coat. The Wind stopped. He could not do it.

Now it was the Sun's turn. The Sun shone gently. He was warm and friendly. Slowly, the man felt the warmth. The man began to feel hot. He opened his coat a little. The Sun shone more. The man felt hotter. Then the man took off his coat and carried it in his hand. The Sun had won.

The lesson of this story
"Gentleness and warmth are often stronger than force." — Forcing someone to do something does not always work. Being kind and gentle is often more powerful.
New words in this story
argument
noun
A disagreement between two or more people. Each person thinks they are right.
"They had an argument about who was right."
contest
noun
A competition or test to find out who is better or stronger.
"They had a contest to see who could run the fastest."
gently
adverb
In a soft, calm, and kind way. Not rough or hard.
"She gently picked up the small bird."
strength
noun
The power to do things physically. How strong something is.
"The wind used all its strength but could not win."
tightly
adverb
Firmly and strongly. Holding something so it cannot move or fall.
"She held the rope tightly so she did not fall."
force
noun/verb
Strong power used to make someone do something they do not want to do.
"You cannot force a person to be happy."
Comprehension questions — write your answers
1. What was the argument between the Wind and the Sun about?
2. Why did the man hold his coat tighter when the Wind blew?
3. Write a sentence using the word "gently".
4. Think of a time someone was kind to you. Write 2 sentences about it.
Story Eight
The Dog and the Bone
A story about being greedy and losing what you have
Week 1–2 level

One day, a dog found a big bone. He was very happy. He picked it up in his mouth and walked home. On the way home, he crossed a small bridge over a river. He looked down into the water. In the water, he saw another dog. That dog also had a big bone in his mouth.

"That dog has a bone too!" thought our dog. "I want that bone. Then I will have two bones." He was greedy. He did not think carefully. He opened his mouth and barked loudly at the dog in the water. He wanted to scare that dog and take his bone.

But when he opened his mouth to bark, his own bone fell into the river. It sank to the bottom. The dog looked in the water again. The other dog was gone too. There was no other dog. It was only his own reflection in the water.

Now the dog had no bone at all. He sat on the bridge and felt very sad. He had been greedy, and he had lost everything.

The lesson of this story
"If you are greedy and want too much, you may lose what you already have." — Be happy with what you have. Wanting more than you need can leave you with nothing.
New words in this story
greedy
adjective
Wanting more than you need. Not happy with what you already have.
"He was greedy and wanted all the food for himself."
reflection
noun
The image you see of yourself in water or a mirror.
"She looked at her reflection in the river."
bark
verb
The sound a dog makes. To make this sound.
"The dog began to bark at the stranger."
sank
verb (past)
Went down to the bottom of water. Base form: sink.
"The stone sank to the bottom of the river."
bridge
noun
A structure built over a river or road so people can cross it.
"We walked across the bridge to the other side."
lost
verb (past)
No longer had something. It was gone. Base form: lose.
"He lost his bag on the way to school."
Comprehension questions — write your answers
1. What did the dog see in the water? What was it really?
2. Why did the dog lose his bone? Use "because" in your answer.
3. Write a sentence with the word "greedy".
4. Write 2 sentences about something you are happy to have in your life.
Story Nine
The Goose and the Golden Egg
A story about greed destroying good things
Week 2–3 level

Once there was a farmer and his wife. They had a goose. Every morning, the goose laid one egg. But this was not a normal egg. It was a golden egg. The farmer took the golden egg to the market and sold it. Every day he got one golden egg, and every day he sold it. He and his wife became wealthy.

But after some time, the farmer was not happy. "One egg a day is too slow," he thought. "I want all the gold now. There must be many golden eggs inside the goose." His wife agreed. They were both very greedy.

So one day, the farmer took a knife and cut open the goose. He looked inside. There were no golden eggs inside. The goose was the same as any other goose inside. He had killed the goose for nothing. Now the goose was dead. There were no more golden eggs. No more eggs at all.

The farmer and his wife sat and cried. They had been foolish. In trying to get everything at once, they had lost everything.

The lesson of this story
"Do not destroy a good thing by wanting too much too fast." — Be patient with good things in your life. If you are greedy and want everything now, you may end up with nothing.
New words in this story
wealthy
adjective
Having a lot of money and valuable things. Rich.
"They became wealthy because of the golden eggs."
laid
verb (past)
Produced an egg. Base form: lay. Used for birds and animals.
"The hen laid three eggs this morning."
foolish
adjective
Not thinking carefully. Making bad decisions without thinking.
"It was foolish to spend all the money in one day."
golden
adjective
Made of gold or the colour of gold. Very valuable.
"The golden egg was very valuable."
patient
adjective
Able to wait calmly without getting angry or upset.
"Be patient — good things take time."
destroy
verb
To damage something so badly that it is gone or broken completely.
"The fire destroyed the whole building."
Comprehension questions — write your answers
1. What did the goose give the farmer every day?
2. Why did the farmer kill the goose? Was this a good idea?
3. Write a sentence using "foolish".
4. Do you have something good in your life that you must take care of? Write 2 sentences.
Story Ten
The Fox and the Crow
A story about flattery and not being fooled by kind words
Week 3–4 level

One day, a crow found a big piece of cheese. She was very happy. She flew up to a branch of a tall tree and held the cheese in her beak. She was ready to eat it.

A fox saw the crow from below. He wanted the cheese. He thought of a plan. He stood under the tree and looked up at the crow. "Good morning, beautiful crow!" said the fox. "You have such magnificent black feathers. Your eyes are bright like stars. You are truly the most beautiful bird I have ever seen."

The crow listened. She felt very happy. She liked these kind words very much. The fox continued. "I am sure that a bird as beautiful as you also has a wonderful voice. Can you sing for me? Please, just one song."

The crow was very flattered. She wanted to show the fox her beautiful voice. She opened her beak wide and began to sing — "Caw! Caw! Caw!" The cheese fell from her beak. It dropped to the ground. The fox jumped on it and ate it quickly. Then he looked up and said, "You have a voice, it is true. But you do not have wisdom." He walked away with a smile.

The lesson of this story
"Do not believe everything nice that people say about you." — When someone gives you many compliments, think carefully. They may want something from you.
New words in this story
magnificent
adjective
Very beautiful and impressive. Much better than normal.
"The palace was magnificent — it was very beautiful."
flattered
adjective/verb
Feeling pleased because someone said something very nice about you.
"She was flattered when he said she was very talented."
wisdom
noun
The ability to make good decisions. Knowledge from experience.
"An old person often has great wisdom."
compliment
noun
Something nice you say to make a person feel good about themselves.
"He gave her a compliment about her cooking."
feathers
noun (plural)
The soft things that cover a bird's body. They help birds fly and stay warm.
"The crow had shiny black feathers."
flattery
noun
Saying very nice things to someone, often to get something from them.
"He used flattery to make her do what he wanted."
Comprehension questions — write your answers
1. What did the fox want? How did he try to get it?
2. Why did the crow open her beak? What happened?
3. Write a sentence using the word "wisdom".
4. Has someone ever said very nice things to you to get something? Write 2 sentences.
Story Eleven
The Two Friends and the Bear
A story about real friendship
Week 2–3 level

Two friends were walking together through a forest. They had been friends for many years. They talked and laughed as they walked. Then suddenly, they heard a loud sound. A big bear was coming toward them through the trees.

The first friend was very scared. He did not think about his friend. He quickly ran to the nearest tree and climbed up. He hid in the branches and did not say a word.

The second friend could not climb trees. He was alone on the path. He thought quickly. He had heard that bears do not touch dead animals. He fell to the ground and lay completely still. He did not move. He did not make a sound. He held his breath.

The bear walked over to him. The bear put his big nose near the man's face and sniffed. The man did not move at all. After a moment, the bear walked away slowly. It thought the man was dead.

The first friend came down from the tree. He tried to smile. "What did the bear whisper to you?" he joked. The second friend looked at him seriously. "The bear told me something important," he said. "He told me never to trust a friend who runs away when you are in danger."

The lesson of this story
"A true friend does not leave you when things are difficult." — You discover who your real friends are when you are in trouble or danger.
New words in this story
whisper
verb/noun
To speak very softly and quietly so only one person can hear.
"She whispered the secret in his ear."
danger
noun
A situation where you could get hurt or something bad could happen.
"The man was in danger when the bear came."
trust
verb/noun
To believe that someone is honest and will help you and not hurt you.
"I trust my best friend with everything."
still
adjective/adverb
Not moving at all. Completely quiet and motionless.
"He lay still on the ground and did not move."
suddenly
adverb
Very quickly and without any warning. Happening fast and unexpectedly.
"Suddenly, a bird flew out of the tree."
discover
verb
To find out something for the first time. To learn something new.
"You discover who your friends are when you need help."
Comprehension questions — write your answers
1. What did the first friend do when the bear came? Was this good?
2. How did the second friend save himself?
3. Write a sentence about your best friend using "trust".
4. What do you think a true friend does? Write 2 sentences starting with "A true friend..."
Story Twelve
The Bundle of Sticks
A story about working together as a family
Week 3–4 level

There was an old farmer. He had three sons. The three sons always quarrelled with each other. They never worked together. The father was very sad about this. He wanted his sons to be united.

One day, the old farmer called his three sons to him. He was very sick and he knew he would die soon. He gave each son a single stick. "Break it," he said. Each son easily broke his stick. Snap! It broke immediately.

Then the father took many sticks and tied them tightly together into a bundle. He gave the bundle to his oldest son. "Now break this," he said. The oldest son tried. He could not break it. He tried very hard but the bundle did not break. The second son tried. He also could not break it. The youngest son tried. He also failed.

The father looked at his sons. "Do you understand?" he said gently. "One stick alone is easy to break. But many sticks together cannot be broken. You are the same. If you fight each other, you are weak. If you work together, nobody can defeat you." The three sons looked at each other. They understood.

The lesson of this story
"Together we are strong. Alone we are weak." — When people work together and help each other, they become much stronger than when they work alone.
New words in this story
quarrelled
verb (past)
Had an angry argument or fight with someone. Base form: quarrel.
"The brothers quarrelled every day about small things."
united
adjective
Joined together as one group. Working together with the same goal.
"The family was united and helped each other always."
bundle
noun
A group of things tied or held together.
"She carried a bundle of wood on her head."
defeat
verb
To beat someone in a fight or competition. To make someone lose.
"Together, they could not be defeated."
weak
adjective
Not strong. Easy to break or hurt. The opposite of strong.
"One stick alone is weak and easy to break."
immediately
adverb
Right away. Without any delay. At once.
"The stick broke immediately — it was very easy."
Comprehension questions — write your answers
1. Why could the sons not break the bundle of sticks?
2. What lesson did the father want to teach? Write 2 sentences.
3. Write a sentence using "united" or "together".
4. Do you work better alone or with other people? Write 2 sentences using "I think..."
Story Thirteen
The Milkmaid and Her Pail
A story about counting your chickens before they hatch
Week 3–4 level

A young milkmaid was walking to the market. She carried a large pail of milk on her head. As she walked, she began to think about all the things she would do with the money from the milk.

"I will sell this milk," she thought. "With the money, I will buy eggs. The eggs will hatch into chickens. I will have many chickens. I will sell the chickens and buy a beautiful dress. When I wear the dress to the market, all the young men will want to talk to me. But I will toss my head and say no to them all." She was very happy with her plan.

She was so happy that she tossed her head proudly — just as she had imagined. But when she tossed her head, the pail of milk fell from her head. It crashed to the ground. All the milk spilled onto the road. The pail was empty. There was no milk, no money, no eggs, no chickens, and no beautiful dress.

The milkmaid sat down on the road and cried. Her mother heard what happened and said, "Do not count your chickens before they hatch, my dear."

The lesson of this story
"Do not make plans based on things that have not happened yet." — It is good to dream, but do not act as if your dreams are already real. First do the work, then enjoy the result.
New words in this story
pail
noun
A round open container with a handle used to carry water or liquid. Also called a bucket.
"She carried the pail of water from the well."
hatch
verb
When a baby animal breaks out of an egg and is born.
"The eggs hatched and five small chickens came out."
spilled

Story Fourteen

Story Twenty
The Peacock and the Crane

A peacock and a crane lived near the same lake. The peacock was very beautiful. He had long, colourful feathers with bright blue and green colours. He was very proud of his beautiful tail. He opened it wide every day for everyone to see.
